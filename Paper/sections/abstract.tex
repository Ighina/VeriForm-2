\begin{abstract}
Large Language Models (LLMs) have shown remarkable improvements in mathematical thinking, especially following the introduction of the Chain-of-Thought paradigm prompting the model to explicitly output the reasoning behind following a problem \emph{step-by-step}.
Following these efforts, previous works have introduced techniques to verify the individual steps of such \emph{reasoning traces}, mostly via asking another stronger LLM or applying supervised fine-tuning of other LLMs judging the individual steps, which itself lead to an opaque verification process. A promising direction avoiding this problem has been explored, consisting of translating each step into deterministically verifiable programs in scripting such as Python or formal languages such as Lean. Critically, in the case of the latter few studies exist and no study address a combination of scripting and formal languages in the context of mathematical verification of reasoning traces from LLMs: this pilot study address this gap in the literature. We show that a router in the form of private or even small open-source LLMs can successfully distringuish whether to translate a mathematical reasoning step into Python or Lean according to its content, proving that combining the two outperforms verifiers based on only one of the two, asa well as most previous baselines. We futher show that the routing can be successfully learned with even smaller models, paving the way to future studies combining several deterministic verifiers at once. 
\end{abstract}
